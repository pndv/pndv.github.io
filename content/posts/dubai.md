_This is part of exercises I took in the writing workshop at Esalen in July 2026._


I am in a taxi in Dubai, tired to my bones. The Pakistani taxi driver looks at his rearview mirror and says in a thick
Punjabi accented English "You look tired", "you must have a 200 plus IQ", I wanted to say, but I merely nodded. Even
nodding was painful. I had spent 3 weeks in a hospital taking care of my mother who had it not been for the doctors and
nurses would not have made it from the medical shock of sepsis.

"I can arrange a massage for you", he said gleefully, only I did not sense the glee. All I heard was "massage" and felt
how badly I needed it. I was nodding enthusiastically, but I had to check into the hotel first. "No problem, sirrr, they
come to your room. Good massage. Verrry good service." There are times when the red flag waves in front of your eyes and
yet you are too exhausted to see it. I asked the driver to make the arrangements and had happily given him my phone
number. I was already imagining a luxurious massage in my own room. I will fall asleep right away. Instruct the masseuse
to close the door behind him. Some comfort, finally. As I finally drift away to sleep.

The call came as I was going up to my room. A lady was squeaking in broken English, "I come now, you come down, we go your room. Tree handradd dollahs". I had dug out the cash from my travel pouch, got into comfortable clothes and was thanking gods
in heaven for making my mother healthy again and finally giving me this treat before I returned to the states.

"I here" the lady messaged on WhatsApp. The anticipation of heavy hands loosening my tight muscles renewed my vigour.
I sprinted to the lobby and started scanning for a lady with a folding massage table. There was only one more person in
the lobby who was approaching me. An Asian girl with a fancy handbag wearing a skirt as short as a bikini bottom and an
open jacket with nothing under it. "Vinniey" she shrieked in a deeply nasal voice. My adrenal glands went into overdrive.
There was no escape. The reception staff was passing glances at me. The lady who checked me in was trying to avoid eye
contact. She could very well have been screaming "couldn't even wait to unpack".

Certainly my intention was to relieve this charming lady of her duties. I searched for my wallet hoping to give her a
crisp $100 note, thank her for coming all the way and, say "no massage" and return her back to the sender postage
pre-paid. Unfortunately, I had left my wallet in my room. It is important to remind my astute readers that I had landed
in Dubai after 3 months of caring for the sick. The mental and physical fatigue therefore had dulled my usual
quick-wittedness.

There are many better ways to come to mind to handle the situation at hand. None came to me at that hour. All I could
think of was to bring her up to my room, give her the money and ask her to leave. I am glad that I am dark; otherwise
everyone could have seen my cheeks were as red as a clown's nose. I could hear the receptionist hammering her
judgemental gavel as I passed through the lobby.

In my room, I gave her the money and asker her to leave. "No massage?" she made suggestive gestures. "No, no, please no"
my ears went scalding hot. "Come on, I give you good service". She was trying to milk even more money out of me. "No,
thank you. Please leave." I opened the door for her.

The next day, passing through the lobby with hurried steps, avoiding any eye contact, I could see through the corner of
my eye the hotel staff grinning at me. It's a hotel, they are in the business of hospitality, they are supposed to
smile. I thought, only to I comforted myself. I couldn't get rid of the nagging thought that in their mind they are
laughing at the fact this chap took a lady upstairs, and the said lady was already returning within minutes of her
arrival...
